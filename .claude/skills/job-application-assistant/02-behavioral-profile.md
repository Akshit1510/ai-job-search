## Overview
Akshit's self-assessment points to a **builder-owner** pattern: someone who does their best work when handed a problem rather than a spec, iterates fast toward something shippable, and wants senior technical depth around them while they do it. Unusually for that profile, they also work well inside structured, predictable delivery environments - the two years at Deutsche Bank are evidence of that - so the fit signal to look for is *autonomy within clear direction*, not chaos.

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive | Level | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Ownership / autonomy | High | Wants end-to-end responsibility for design and delivery; specs handed down without context are demotivating |
| Pace / iteration | High | Energized by short cycles, POCs, and experiments - the AI POCs at Deutsche Bank were self-driven |
| Learning depth | High | Actively seeks strong senior engineers and code-review culture; values technical depth over process |

## Strongest Behaviors
- **End-to-end ownership:** Led the backend of a regulatory trade-exception platform, from REST API design through releases and CI/CD, rather than owning a slice.
- **Self-directed initiative:** Built AI POCs (NL-to-SQL with LangGraph, enterprise RAG chatbot) that were not the assigned core work, and carried them far enough to demonstrate value.
- **Performance instinct:** Repeatedly finds and fixes the bottleneck - 30-40% faster request flows, ~35% faster SQL, ~40-50% faster ingestion via multithreading. Optimization is a reflex, not a task.
- **Debugging persistence:** Diagnosed and resolved embedding-corruption issues in production ingestion pipelines - a hard, unglamorous class of bug.
- **Collaboration and mentoring:** Recognized for cross-functional collaboration and team mentoring; holds a Team Excellence Award (2025).

## How You Work Best
- Given a problem and the autonomy to design the solution, with clear direction on *what matters* and latitude on *how*
- On teams that ship frequently and tolerate experiments and POCs
- With strong senior engineers available for technical mentorship and real code review
- With enough process that priorities are stable and work does not get discarded mid-flight
- On work with genuine technical depth - **either** substantial backend and systems engineering (scale, latency, architecture ownership) **or** AI/LLM work with a real path to production rather than stopping at demo. These two are equally motivating; a role does not need both.

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "ownership", "end-to-end", "autonomy", "you'll own"
- "fast-paced", "ship quickly", "iterate", "0-to-1", "prototype to production"
- "strong engineering culture", "code review", "mentorship", "learn from senior engineers"
- "high-scale", "performance", "optimization", "low latency", "distributed systems", "API design", "microservices", "throughput"
- "LLM in production", "applied AI", "RAG", "agentic systems"

Both of those lines carry **equal weight**. A posting that hits the first line hard and never mentions AI is as strong a fit as one that hits the second.

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breaker):
- "ambiguous", "undefined scope", "you'll figure out what to build", "wear many hats" with no anchor role
- "client-facing delivery", "onsite at client", "staffing", "resource augmentation" - this crosses into the recorded deal-breaker
- "maintain legacy systems", "L2/L3 support", "production support rotation" as the primary responsibility
- Heavy governance language with no counterweight ("multiple approval layers", "change advisory board") - Akshit already has this at Deutsche Bank and is not seeking more of it

## Management Style Preferences
- **Works well with:** managers who set clear outcomes and get out of the way; technically credible leads who can review work substantively; managers who protect the team from churn
- **Doesn't work well with:** micromanagement of implementation detail; managers who change direction frequently without explaining why; purely non-technical management with no one senior to learn from
